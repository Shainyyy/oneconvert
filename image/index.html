Choosing the Right Image Format

Three formats cover almost everything, and the choice follows from the content rather than from any general ranking. JPEG is built for photographs: its compression discards detail your eye is unlikely to miss, which works beautifully on natural texture and gradual gradients and badly on sharp edges. It has no transparency. PNG is lossless and supports an alpha channel, making it correct for screenshots, logos, diagrams, line art and anything with flat colour or hard edges — but on a photograph it produces a file several times the size of an equivalent JPEG. WebP covers both cases, with a lossy mode that beats JPEG by roughly 25–35% at matched quality and a lossless mode that beats PNG by 20–30%, and it keeps transparency in both.

The practical rule: photographs go to JPEG or lossy WebP, graphics and screenshots to PNG or lossless WebP, and anything needing transparency avoids JPEG entirely. WebP is the better technical choice for the web today, since browser support has been universal for years; the reason to keep JPEG and PNG around is everything that is not a browser — older desktop software, print workflows, upload forms and devices that still reject the format.

One principle applies across every conversion here: lossy compression is not reversible. Converting a JPEG to PNG stores its existing artefacts losslessly but cannot remove them, and each lossy re-encode adds a new generation of loss on top of the last. Keep the highest-quality original you have as your master and treat every converted file as a disposable copy you can regenerate. If you find yourself converting the same image back and forth between formats, you are compounding damage with each round trip.
